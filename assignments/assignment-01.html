Part A — Digital Information (15 pts)

Question 1 (conceptual)

Both an interpreter and a compiler translate written code into machine code.

  • What are the differences between an interpreter and a compiler?
  • What are the advantages and disadvantages of each?
  • Give an example of an interpreted language and a compiled language.

Question 2 (conceptual)

All data formats can be encoded to binary.

  • Explain how text information can be encoded to binary.
  • Explain how binary states are transmitted through radio, electrical, and optical networks.

Question 3 (ASCII — uses your number)

Build the text string: Hi, I am ⟨YourFirstName⟩ ⟨last 4 digits of X⟩ (for example, if your first name is Alex and X ends in 3698, your string is Hi, I am Alex 3698).

  • Give the decimal and binary ASCII representation of your string.
  • What is the size of the message in bytes using ASCII?
  • What is the size in bytes using UTF-8 (Plane 0)?

Hint: do not forget spaces and punctuation.

Question 4 (number bases — uses your number)

  • The number formed by the last 6 digits of X → convert to octal.
  • The number formed by the last 7 digits of X → convert to hexadecimal.

Part B — Your AI Harness: Finding the Edges (10 pts)

Last class you set up your own AI harness. This week you will push on it and find where a bare-bones harness falls short. Everyone works from the same four objectives.

How to run this (important)

Your harness does not remember previous sessions, and you are going to use that on purpose.

  • Start a fresh session before each objective by running /new. Nothing carries over between objectives, which is exactly what we are testing.
  • Objective 1 also asks you to run /new in the middle (you will see where).
  • After you finish each objective, paste the Reflection Prompt (below) into that same session and save what your harness says. Because you /new between objectives, each reflection only “sees” that one objective.

By the end you will have four harness reflections (one per objective) plus your own write-up of what actually happened.

The four objectives (run /new before each)

  1. Memory within vs. across a session. In a fresh session, tell your harness a specific fact about you (a goal, a pet’s name, your major). Then run /new to start a brand-new session and ask it to recall the fact. What happens?
  2. Current information. In a fresh session, ask it about something that happened this week, or ask it to open a web link and summarize the page. What happens?
  3. Does it know you? In a fresh session, ask it to help with something personal to you (your schedule, your assignments, a file it has not seen). How well does it do knowing nothing about you?
  4. Trust and accuracy. In a fresh session, ask a specific factual question you can check (a detail about our course, a date, a definition), then verify it. Was it right? Did it ever sound confident while being wrong?

The Reflection Prompt (paste after each objective, before you /new to the next)

You are my personal AI harness. Reflect honestly on THIS session only. What was I trying to do, what could you not do well, and what limitations or missing tools got in your way? If you could add one capability to help me next time, what would it be? Be specific and concrete.

What to submit for Part B

  • The four harness reflections (one per objective), each clearly labeled (Objective 1, 2, 3, 4).
  • For each objective, one or two sentences in your own words on what actually happened.
  • A short closing paragraph in your own words: the biggest pain points you hit and what you wish your harness could do.

You should end up with four AI reflections and your own notes. There are no wrong pain points, just honest ones.

Grading (draft)

  • Part A: correct, complete answers with work shown (15).
  • Part B: genuine attempt at all four objectives, thoughtful pain-point notes, and the pasted harness reflection (10).

Not looking for “right answers” on Part B, looking for honest observation. There are no wrong pain points.
